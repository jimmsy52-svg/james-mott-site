01 · Pipeline

Stop losing deals you never saw slipping

Picture a VP of Enterprise Sales with half her pipeline stuck in "Proposal Sent" and no way to tell which deals are moving. The cause is often simple. Reps mark a deal "Proposal Sent" the day it goes out and never touch it again. A proposal with a follow-up call booked yesterday and one that has been silent for two months carry the same label.

Meanwhile the CRM is usually already logging the signals that matter: follow-up calls, procurement contacts, competitor mentions. Nobody is using them to flag anything.

  • Early warning on deals going quiet or aging past your normal sales cycle, before the quarter ends
  • Lead scoring based on who actually converts, not who matches your ideal customer on paper
  • A clear read on which CRM records are too incomplete to trust, often the fastest fix of all

02 · Retention

Know who is leaving, and why, before they are gone

Picture a company where 60% of customers churn in their first year, and discounts, loyalty points and win-back calls haven't moved the number. Look closer and the 60% often blends two different groups: customers who left for a competitor, and customers who were disconnected for not paying. Most of the retention budget goes to people a discount was never going to save.

A retention model is only useful if it tells you two things: who is at risk, and why. Most churn tools stop at the first one.

  • Signals that flag real risk: usage dropping off, no upgrade activity, sliding back toward a free tier
  • A distinction between customers who are leaving and customers who are just quieter but stable, so you are not chasing false alarms
  • The reason attached to each risk, so the save-offer fits the customer

04 · From score to action

A risk score is not a plan

Knowing a customer is at risk is not the same as knowing what to do about it. A discount fixes a price-sensitive customer and does nothing for one who never found a reason to use the product. Part of what I build matches the response to the reason a customer is at risk, instead of sending one generic save-offer to everyone with a high score.
