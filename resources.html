From Card Catalogs to ChatGPT

Knox College · with moderator Mark Stefanik ’74

A personal reflection on fifty years of research transformation, from the card catalogs and microfiche of 1974 to today’s AI assistants. Drawing on my own dissertation work with Gallup polling archives, the talk asks what the slow, effortful research of the pre-digital era actually taught us, what we’ve gained in the shift to Google and ChatGPT, and what we’ve quietly traded away.

Three things it covers
  • What the card-catalog era actually taught researchers, and why the effort mattered
  • Three live AI demos on my 1936 election data
  • The CRAAP test as a working filter for sources in the AI age
