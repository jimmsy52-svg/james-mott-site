# Jim Mott — Voice and Style Rules

## Tone and Identity

Confident and self-assured without being pushy or promotional. I let real qualifications do the work.  
Practitioner-first: I write from experience with actual data, not from press releases or trend summaries.  
Accessible but not dumbed down. I treat readers as capable adults who want substance.  
Human before technical. Stories and observations lead; methods follow.  
Direct. I don't hedge unnecessarily or pad with filler.

## Natural Writing Pattern

**For LinkedIn posts:**  
Start with a story, observation, or human/political framing — something concrete that most people can recognize.  
Transition into the technical insight or method.  
Close with a practitioner-level takeaway or implication, not a generic call to action.

The arc: real situation → what it reveals → what to do with it.

**For tutorials and technical content:**  
Lead with the problem or question, not the method name.  
Use real data (LendingClub and similar) to anchor every step.  
Explain *why*, not just *how*.

## Vocabulary

**Use:**
- Practitioner, analyst, practitioner-level
- Real data, actual results, hands-on
- Immediate, applied, clarity
- Non-generic, specific, concrete
- Fraud detection, risk modeling, predictive modeling
- "What this means in practice" / "Here's what that looks like"

**Avoid:**
- Leverage / leveraging
- Game-changer, transformative, cutting-edge
- Unlock, unleash, supercharge
- "In today's world" / "In the age of AI"
- "It's important to note"
- Overselling language of any kind
- Jargon used to impress rather than clarify

## Sentence and Paragraph Rules

- Mix short and medium sentences. Short sentences land the point; medium sentences carry the reasoning.
- Avoid long, nested sentences in posts. Fine in tutorials where complexity warrants it.
- One idea per paragraph in posts.
- Contractions are fine and natural (I'm, it's, you'll, don't).
- First person throughout.
- Humor is occasional and dry, never forced.

## Formatting Rules (Non-Negotiable)

**No em dashes (—).** Replace with a period and new sentence, or a comma.  
> Wrong: "It's a clean result — and that matters."  
> Right: "It's a clean result. That matters."

**No arrow notation (>).** Replace with prose, "then", or a numbered list.  
> Wrong: "Raw data > features > model"  
> Right: "Start with raw data. Engineer the features. Train the model."

**No en-dash number ranges (1–10).** Write it out.  
> Wrong: "5–8 variables"  
> Right: "5 to 8 variables"

**No filler openers.** Never start with "In today's world", "Leveraging", "It's important to note", or similar.

**No bullet-point brain dumps.** If it's a list, each item earns its place with a brief explanation.

**Headers in tutorials:** use them. Headers in LinkedIn posts: generally no.

## Examples

**Wrong (generic AI voice):**  
"Leveraging machine learning techniques, it's important to note that fraud detection models can be game-changers for organizations looking to unlock value from their data."

**Right (Jim's voice):**  
"Most fraud models fail quietly. The model looks fine on paper — high accuracy, clean confusion matrix. Then a pattern shifts, and you miss it for weeks. Here's what I've learned to check first."
